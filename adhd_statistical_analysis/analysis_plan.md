# Analysis specification

Specified on 2026-10-20, after source and scoring inspection and before running this project's inferential calculations. This is a task-specific analysis specification, not a registered prospective study protocol. The task itself supplies approximate Israeli p-values and two conflicting historical South African results; those expectations were not used to choose methods.

1. Primary family: Sarid & Lipka (2023), COVID-19 remote-learning sample, ADHD-only (n=68) versus no LD/ADHD (n=197). Two two-sided Welch tests: academic adjustment and social adjustment. Null: population mean difference = 0; directional research hypothesis: ADHD-only mean is lower. Alpha=.05, Holm across these two tests. Differences and Hedges' g are ADHD minus comparison. Report unadjusted 95% confidence intervals and clearly distinguish them from multiplicity-adjusted p-values.
2. Source A is a continuous symptom study, not a diagnosed ADHD/control comparison. Documented ASRS inattention items: 1–4 and 7–11; hyperactivity/impulsivity: 5–6 and 12–18. Require complete items; no new imputation, dichotomization, winsorization, or outlier deletion.
3. Source A's baseline AAS stored total has an unresolved reverse-coding issue. Its 18 partial-item totals are not comparable to complete nine-item totals. Use the 488 complete-item records for a **provisional numerical audit**, not confirmatory evidence of a validated adjustment construct. Family: Pearson associations of ASRS total, inattention, and hyperactivity/impulsivity with the author-supplied complete-item AAS total (Holm across three). Spearman sensitivity tests have a separate three-test family; bootstrap percentile CIs use seed 42 and 5,000 paired resamples. No alternative AAS recoding is inferred from item correlations.
4. The historical inattention/AAS coefficient is audited using the documented inattention sum and complete AAS cases. A rejected first-nine-items ASRS sum is computed only to check a plausible historical scoring error. It is not an ADHD inattention measure, is not included in hypothesis tests, and is not used to select the preferred result.
5. Source A's verified weighted first-semester GPA, individual IDs, waves 2/3 and item-level codebook are absent from the public workbook. Do not relabel the single-course `psy1004_grade` as GPA. Do not infer meanings for absent `b1`/`b2`. Exclude `asrs1_total.y` and `aas_change` from inference: the former conflicts with its label; the latter cannot be reconstructed from the released wave data. GPA correlations/regression and longitudinal modeling remain unavailable.
6. A provisional AAS regression uses ASRS total plus age, Grade 12 mark and `nbt_ave`, specified from the article's covariates. Use complete cases and HC3 robust standard errors. A second, explanatory sensitivity model adds BDI-II only. Do not use the discrepant BAI stored total or the modified AUDIT as covariates. Holm across the two ASRS coefficients. These are exploratory score audits, not GPA regressions or causal models. Inspect VIFs, residuals, leverage and Cook's distance without deleting records.
7. Gormley (2019), Lipka (2020), and Baczewski (2022) provide context, including contrary findings. Do not pool different diagnoses, outcomes, ages, semesters, or populations. Do not add exploratory group tests for context papers. Kim (2015) is optional and is excluded because schoolchildren and high-risk screening groups do not directly address the university diagnosis comparison.
8. Raw-source files remain unchanged. Raw distributions, missingness, ranges, duplicate checks, scoring checks and attrition are recorded. Published summaries receive only computable statistics; individual-level medians, quartiles, outliers, normality and variance tests are unavailable. No synthetic participants are created.
